dont use variable - signals are better as they're always sintetizzabili. if you use variable in the project you'll get asked why instead of signals.

use constants when needed, not signals that remain unchanged (error)

no multi clock domains - project is doable with a single clock signal

even though the technical part is the most fun (you do something) the report is the part on which you are graded and evalutated (especially in workplace). note also that the assignment is a contract between you and the client, so every request is bonding - you can't just not do it, or you won't be graded (paid) fully.

the easier (to read) the report the better it is.

liability is fundamental - you can obv use something not made by you (or we'll be reinventing the wheel) but you're liable for it. so must know it and understand it deeply, cause a problem with it is your responsability.

in the report code quality > quantity - so just a few snippets but with high logical value. same works for assertions, they must be quantifiable (eg, it works bettere -> it has a X% performance boost).

test normal condition and limit cases - no need to test every possibile inout but the most significants.


global signals (coming from outside and common to everyone), most importantly reset and clock, shouldn't pass through simple logic but go directly to the components. this is because even a single gate introduces delay, and these signals must have the lowest delay between components as possible. moral: don't negate reset if it's active high and you want it low, declare it before (or adapt).


must manage my warnings (in vivado). red=errors, orange=criticals (these shouldn't be there). yellow=warning - i must decide if i can ignore it or it. if it is ignorable must spec in the report why it is, if it isn't ignorable it means you have to solve it


slack - the analysis in vivado is already referred to the worst case. this means that until it is positive we have, even in the worst case, some margin. it is also performed on Register Logic Register, all the paths that are floating (signals not ending in registers) are automatically discarded


power - static is used by chip to stay on, independent from our design. the dynamic power consumption depend on our design. IO is the highest one (usually). this power consumptions are estimates made by vivado simulating a 50% usage. real power consumption depends on application's use.
